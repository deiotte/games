Two is One

A Redundancy Strategy Game

In security and operations, there's a saying: "Two is one, and one is none." If you have only one of something critical, you effectively have zero — because it will fail when you need it most.

But the saying doesn't tell you when to stop adding backups. At what point does more redundancy create more risk?

How to play:

  • You manage 5 critical services over 30 days
  • Each day, spend your budget to add instances (redundant copies) to services
  • You can also diversify a service to reduce correlated failure risk
  • Click Run Day to simulate — components fail randomly
  • A service goes OFFLINE if all its instances fail at once
  • Keep your reputation above zero to survive

Three types of failure:

  • Independent — each instance can fail on its own (20% per day). More instances = less risk.
  • Correlated — a common cause takes out ALL instances at once (5%). Diversifying reduces this.
  • Complexity — too many instances (4+) creates management overhead that causes its own failures.

Find the sweet spot. Pure redundancy has limits.
